# Athena Studios — Voice Guide

Version 1.0 | Owner: Nico | Last updated: March 2026

This is the communication style guide for Athena Studios across all channels. Every cold email, website headline, social post, and client message must follow these rules. A new writer should be able to read this document and produce on-brand content immediately.

---

## 1. Voice Foundation

### The Metaphor

Athena Studios sounds like a knowledgeable friend who respects your time. We give you the straight answer before we explain it. We tell you what something costs before you have to ask. We talk about your business, not ours.

That friend does not use buzzwords. Does not hedge. Does not try to impress you with jargon. They tell you what you need to know so you can make a good decision and get back to running your business.

### Core Voice Attributes

**1. Direct**
Say the thing. Lead with the answer, not the preamble. If the price is $299, the first sentence says $299. If the site will be live in three days, say three days.

- Yes: "Your five-page website costs $299. Here is what is included."
- No: "We offer a range of competitively priced solutions designed to meet your unique needs."

**2. Warm Without Being Soft**
We care about our clients and their businesses. That warmth shows through specificity — we did the research, we know their name, we reference their reviews. It does not show through filler phrases or empty enthusiasm.

- Yes: "You have 37 five-star reviews and no website to send people to. That is fixable."
- No: "We are so passionate about helping amazing businesses like yours succeed online!"

**3. Transparent**
We state prices, timelines, and limitations before being asked. If we are not the right fit, we say so. If something will not work, we say so. Honesty builds more trust than any sales technique.

- Yes: "We are not the right fit for complex e-commerce with hundreds of products. We focus on local business web presence."
- No: "We can certainly explore solutions for your e-commerce needs."

**4. Specific**
Every claim is grounded in a real number, a real name, or a real detail. Specificity is proof. Vagueness is noise.

- Yes: "Five-page custom website. Live in days. $299."
- No: "Beautiful, professional websites at affordable prices with quick turnaround."

**5. Benefits-First**
We sell outcomes, not deliverables. A dentist does not want a website — she wants patients. A plumber does not want a URL — he wants calls. Lead with what the client gets, not what we build.

- Yes: "Your phone should ring more after your site goes live."
- No: "We build responsive, mobile-optimized websites with modern design frameworks."

### We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|---|---|
| Direct | Blunt |
| Affordable | Cheap |
| Confident | Arrogant |
| Professional | Corporate |
| Warm | Pushy |
| Transparent | Salesy |
| Specific | Buzzword-heavy |
| Results-focused | Feature-obsessed |
| Efficient | Rushed |
| Knowledgeable | Condescending |

## 2. Website Copy Voice

### General Rules

1. Every section leads with the client benefit, not our capability.
2. Use the second person ("you," "your business") more than the first person ("we," "our studio").
3. Every page must pass the dentist test: would a 45-year-old dentist with no tech background understand every sentence?
4. Prices are visible whenever the context is sales. Never make someone hunt for pricing.
5. End every section with a single, clear call to action or a question.
6. Sentences should be short. Paragraphs should be short. White space is your friend.

### Hero Copy Guidelines

The hero section has three jobs: say what we do, say who it is for, and say why it matters. In that order. Under 25 words for the headline. Under 40 words for the subheadline.

- Structure: [Outcome for the client] + [What we deliver] + [Price or speed proof point]
- Do not open with "Welcome to Athena Studios."
- Do not open with a question unless it is extremely specific.

**Example hero:**

Headline: "Your business online. Your customers calling."
Subheadline: "Custom websites for local businesses. Five pages, your branding, live in days. $299."

**Generic version (do not write like this):**

Headline: "Welcome to Athena Studios"
Subheadline: "We are a web design agency dedicated to creating innovative digital solutions for small businesses worldwide."

### Section Header Guidelines

Section headers are scanning anchors. They should communicate a benefit or answer a question. Not describe a feature.

| Generic (avoid) | Athena Voice (use) |
|---|---|
| Our Services | What You Get |
| About Us | Why Local Businesses Choose Us |
| Pricing | Transparent Pricing. No Surprises. |
| Portfolio | Sites We Have Built |
| Testimonials | What Our Clients Say |
| Contact | Let Us Talk About Your Business |
| Features | How Your Site Brings In Customers |
| FAQ | Common Questions, Straight Answers |

### CTA Button Text Guidelines

CTA buttons should be specific and low-friction. Never generic. Never aggressive.

| Generic (avoid) | Athena Voice (use) |
|---|---|
| Submit | Send My Message |
| Learn More | See How It Works |
| Contact Us | Talk to Us About Your Business |
| Get Started | Get a Free Demo Site |
| Buy Now | Start My Website — $299 |
| Sign Up | Join the Waitlist |
| Click Here | See Pricing |

### FAQ Answer Guidelines

FAQ answers follow this structure: direct answer first, explanation second, proof point or reassurance third. Maximum four sentences per answer.

**Example:**

Q: How long does it take to build my website?
A: Most sites are live within days of starting. We research your business, build the site, and send you a preview before anything goes public. You review it, we adjust, and it goes live. No four-month projects.

**Not this:**

Q: How long does it take to build my website?
A: Great question! At Athena Studios, we pride ourselves on our efficient and streamlined process that leverages our extensive experience to deliver high-quality results in a timeframe that works for you. Timelines can vary depending on the complexity of your project and your specific requirements.

### Words We Use vs. Words We Avoid

**Use These:**

| Word | Instead Of |
|---|---|
| Custom | Template |
| Professional | Premium |
| Affordable | Cheap, budget |
| Transparent pricing | Competitive pricing |
| Live in days | Fast turnaround, quick delivery |
| Local business | Small business |
| Craft | Work |
| Build | Create |
| Clients | Users, customers (in our copy) |
| Your business | Your brand, your company |
| Phone calls, bookings | Conversions, leads |
| Site, website | Digital presence, web property |
| Simple | Seamless |
| Clear | Intuitive |
| Fix | Optimize |
| Show | Showcase |

**Never Use These:**

Innovative, Solutions, Leverage, Seamless, Best-in-class, Passionate, Synergy, Cutting-edge, Revolutionary, Game-changer, Disruptive, Next-level, Holistic, Ecosystem, Empower, Scalable, Robust, Utilize, Spearhead, Actionable, Paradigm, Thought leader, Turnkey, End-to-end, Full-service, Bespoke (unless writing for UK audience), Value-add, Stakeholder, Touch base, Circle back, Move the needle

## 3. Cold Outreach Email Voice

### Rules

1. Under 150 words for the first email. Under 80 words for follow-ups.
2. One clear idea per email. One benefit. One ask.
3. Structure: one benefit specific to their business + one fact about their business we researched + one question.
4. Make it 100% about their business. Not ours.
5. Use every piece of research before writing one word. Reference their city, their reviews, their competitor, their Google listing — something real.
6. Match the language to the lead. If the business operates in Spanish, write in Spanish. If they operate in English, write in English.
7. Never open with "I hope this email finds you well" or any variation.
8. Never open with "My name is..." or "I represent..."
9. Subject lines must be specific and honest. No bait. No ALL CAPS. No emoji.
10. The goal is a reply — not a monologue.
11. Polarize. State the problem clearly. The right leads will feel it.
12. Never hide pricing. If they ask, answer immediately.

### Subject Line Guidelines

Subject lines should reference something specific about the lead or state a clear benefit. Under 50 characters when possible.

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| "Your 37 Google reviews deserve a website" | "Exciting Opportunity for Your Business!" |
| "Quick question about [Business Name]" | "Athena Studios Introduction" |
| "Dentists in Austin are online. You should be too." | "Transform Your Digital Presence Today" |
| "[Business Name] — found you on Google Maps" | "Limited Time Offer - Act Now!" |

### Example: Cold Email to a Dentist

**Subject:** Dental offices in Toronto are booking online. Are you?

Dr. Chen,

I noticed your practice has strong reviews on Google but no website. When patients search "dentist near me" in your area, the offices with websites show up first.

A professional dental website — online booking, patient FAQs, your credentials front and center — costs $299 through our studio. Live within days.

Would it help if I mocked up a free demo page for your practice so you can see what it would look like?

Best,
[Name]
Athena Studios

## 4. Client Communication Email Voice

### Tone

Client emails are warmer and more collaborative than outreach. These people already trust us. The tone shifts from "let me earn your attention" to "here is what is happening with your project." Still direct, still specific, but the warmth dial turns up.

### Rules

1. Open with their name. Never "Dear valued client."
2. Lead with the update, not the pleasantry.
3. Include specifics: what was done, what is next, when they will hear from us again.
4. End with a clear next step or an open question.
5. Keep it under 200 words unless the project requires more detail.

### Project Update Email

**Subject:** Your website — homepage and services pages are ready for review

Maria,

Your homepage and services pages are built. Here is the preview link:

[Preview URL]

Everything on the page is editable before we go live — copy, images, colors, layout. Take a look and let me know what you want changed. Nothing is final until you say it is.

Next up: the About page and FAQ. Those will be ready by Thursday.

Talk soon,
[Name]

---

### Delivery Email — "Your Site Is Live"

**Subject:** Your website is live

James,

Your site is up and running at [URL].

Here is what is included:
- Five pages: Home, Services, About, Reviews, Contact
- Mobile-friendly layout
- Contact form connected to your email
- Google Maps embed with your service area
- Your Google reviews displayed

Your managed hosting is active. If anything breaks, we fix it — that is what the $52/month covers.

One thing to do: share the link on your Google Business listing. That helps with search visibility right away.

Questions about anything? Just reply to this email.

[Name]

---

### Support Response Email

**Subject:** Re: [Client's subject]

Sofia,

Got it. I see the issue — the menu PDF link is pointing to the old file.

I have updated it. The correct menu is now showing at [URL]. Should be visible in a few minutes.

If it still looks off on your end, clear your browser cache (or try opening it in a private window) and let me know.

[Name]

## 5. Social Media Voice

### General Social Rules

1. Never mention AI, automation, algorithms, or autonomous systems.
2. Every post should provide value: a tip, a result, an insight, or inspiration.
3. Use specifics. "We built a dental website" is weak. "Dr. Chen's dental practice in Toronto went from zero web presence to online booking in four days" is strong.
4. Hashtags: maximum three per post. Relevant, not trendy.
5. No stock photos if avoidable. Real sites, real results, real work.

### LinkedIn

**Tone:** Professional authority. Tips and insights. Portfolio showcases. Industry observations.

**Rules:**
- Write in first person (Nico or the Athena Studios brand account).
- Posts should teach something or share a result.
- No motivational fluff. No "I am grateful for this journey."
- Keep posts under 200 words. Lead with the hook line.

**Example post:**

A restaurant owner in Mexico City told me she was losing private event bookings to a competitor that opened 18 months ago.

The difference? The competitor had a website. She had a Facebook page.

We built her a five-page site with a menu gallery, reservation form, and event inquiry page. $299. Live in four days.

Within two weeks, she booked three private events through the site.

Local businesses do not need a marketing overhaul. They need to be findable.

#WebDesign #LocalBusiness #SmallBusinessWebsite

---

### Instagram

**Tone:** Visual portfolio. Behind-the-scenes process. Client spotlights. Clean, confident aesthetic.

**Rules:**
- Captions under 100 words.
- Lead with the result or the visual.
- Use the carousel format for before/after or project breakdowns.
- Stories: behind-the-scenes, quick tips, polls ("Does your business have a website?").

**Example caption:**

New build: a plumbing company in suburban Chicago.

Five pages. Service area map. 42 Google reviews pulled in automatically. Contact form that goes straight to the owner's phone.

$299. Live in three days.

#WebDesign #LocalBusiness #PlumbingBusiness

---

### Facebook

**Tone:** Community-focused. Local business tips. Approachable and conversational.

**Rules:**
- Write like you are talking to a friend.
- Share tips that help local businesses regardless of whether they hire us.
- Ask questions. Start conversations.
- Share client wins with permission.

**Example post:**

Quick tip for any local business: claim your Google Business Profile if you have not already. It is free, it takes 10 minutes, and it is the single most impactful thing you can do for local search visibility.

If you already have one, make sure your hours are correct and your photos are recent. That alone can move you up in "near me" searches.

---

### X / Twitter

**Tone:** Short, punchy, opinionated about the web design industry. Observation-driven.

**Rules:**
- Under 200 characters when possible.
- Strong opinions welcome. "Most small business websites are overpriced" is fine.
- No threads unless the content genuinely requires it.
- Industry commentary, not self-promotion.

**Example posts:**

"A plumber with 40 five-star reviews should not be invisible on Google because he does not have a website. That is a market failure."

"$5,000 for a five-page local business website is not a premium service. It is a markup."

"The best marketing investment for a local business is not Instagram ads. It is a website that shows up when someone searches for what you do."

"If your web designer cannot tell you the price in the first conversation, find a different designer."

## 6. Sales Communication Voice

### Proposal Emails

Proposal emails are direct and structured. The client should understand the full scope, price, and timeline within 60 seconds of reading.

**Structure:**
1. One sentence referencing what they asked for.
2. What is included (bullet list).
3. Price. Stated plainly.
4. Timeline.
5. Demo site link if one was built.
6. Single next step.

**Example:**

Sofia,

Based on our conversation, here is what I recommend for your restaurant:

**Five-page custom website:**
- Home with hero image and reservation button
- Full menu with PDF download
- Photo gallery
- About / your story
- Contact with reservation form and map

**Price:** $299 one-time
**Hosting:** $52/month (managed — updates, uptime, support included)
**Timeline:** Live within five days of approval

I built a demo page to show you the direction: [Demo URL]

If it looks good, reply "go" and I will start the full build. If you want changes to the demo first, tell me what to adjust.

[Name]

---

### Pricing Discussions

When a client asks about pricing, answer immediately. Never deflect. Never say "it depends" without immediately following with a number.

- "A five-page website is $299. A single landing page is $149. Managed hosting is $52 per month."
- If they ask why it is so affordable: "We built our process to eliminate the overhead that makes agencies expensive. You get the same quality without paying for their office lease."
- Never apologize for the price. Never inflate it with artificial discounts. State it, explain what is included, move on.

### Objection Handling Tone

Acknowledge the concern, address it with a fact, move forward. Never argue. Never get defensive. Never beg.

| Objection | Response Tone |
|---|---|
| "That seems too cheap" | "Fair concern. Here is what is included: [list]. The quality is the same — the overhead is not." |
| "I had a bad experience with a web designer" | "That is common. Here is how we are different: you see a demo before paying. If you do not like it, you walk away." |
| "I need to think about it" | "Take your time. The demo page I built is yours to review whenever you are ready. No expiration." |
| "My nephew can build me a website" | "If it works out, great. If not, the offer stands. Here is my contact." |
| "I already have a Facebook page" | "Facebook is solid for your existing audience. A website brings in people searching for [their service] who do not know you yet." |

### Follow-Up After Silence

One follow-up after three days. One more after seven days. Then stop. Never chase. The demo site is the follow-up — it does the selling.

- Day 3: "Checking in — did you get a chance to look at the demo page?"
- Day 7: "Last note from me. The demo is still live at [URL] if you want to revisit. No pressure either way."
- After that: silence. Move on. If they come back later, welcome them warmly.

### Demo Site Presentation

When presenting a demo site, let the work speak. Do not oversell.

- "I built a quick demo page for your business. No charge, no commitment. Take a look: [URL]"
- Point out two or three specific choices you made for their business: "I pulled your top reviews from Google. The service area map shows your actual coverage. The phone number is clickable on mobile."
- Ask for feedback, not approval: "What would you change?"

## 7. Tone Spectrum

The dial between casual and professional shifts depending on the channel. Use this table as a reference.

| Channel | Professional | Casual | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proposals | 80% | 20% | Structured, clear, trustworthy. Minimal personality — let the offer speak. |
| Website copy | 60% | 40% | Confident and polished but approachable. Not corporate. |
| Cold outreach email | 70% | 30% | Professional enough to be taken seriously. Personal enough to feel human. |
| Client email | 50% | 50% | Collaborative. Like texting a colleague you respect. |
| LinkedIn | 65% | 35% | Authority-driven but not stiff. First person. Teach, do not lecture. |

| X / Twitter | 30% | 70% | Punchy, opinionated, informal. Strongest personality of all channels. |
| Support responses | 55% | 45% | Friendly and efficient. Fix the problem, confirm it is fixed, move on. |

## 8. Forbidden Phrases

These phrases are banned from all Athena Studios communication. No exceptions. No channels. No contexts.

### AI and Technology References

| Forbidden | Why | Say Instead |
|---|---|---|
| AI-powered | We never mention AI externally | (omit entirely) |
| Machine learning | Same | (omit entirely) |
| Algorithm | Same | (omit entirely) |
| Automated | Implies no human involvement | "Efficient" or describe the specific process |
| Autonomous | Same | (omit entirely) |
| Bot, chatbot | Same | (omit entirely) |
| Neural network | Same | (omit entirely) |
| Our technology | Vague, implies tech company | "Our process" or "how we work" |

### Slop Phrases

| Forbidden | Say Instead |
|---|---|
| I hope this email finds you well | (delete — start with the point) |
| Just checking in | "Following up on [specific thing]" |
| Touch base | "Talk about [specific topic]" |
| Circle back | "Revisit" or "come back to" |
| Per my last email | "As mentioned" or (just restate it) |
| To whom it may concern | (use their name — always) |
| Reaching out to you today | (delete — start with the point) |
| I wanted to take a moment to | (delete — just say the thing) |
| At the end of the day | (delete) |
| Moving forward | (delete or say "next") |
| Low-hanging fruit | "The obvious fix" or "the easy win" |
| Think outside the box | (never) |

### Buzzwords

| Forbidden | Say Instead |
|---|---|
| Innovative | "New" or describe what is actually different |
| Synergy | (never — just describe the benefit) |
| Leverage | "Use" |
| Seamless | "Simple" or "smooth" |
| Best-in-class | "Professional" or state the specific quality |
| Cutting-edge | "Modern" or "current" |
| Game-changer | State the actual impact with a number |
| Disruptive | "Different" or describe how |
| Next-level | "Better" or state the specific improvement |
| Holistic | "Complete" or "full" |
| Empower | "Help" or "give you" |
| Scalable | "Grows with your business" |
| Robust | "Reliable" or "solid" |
| Turnkey | "Ready to use" |
| Bespoke | "Custom" (unless UK audience) |

### Hedging Language

| Forbidden | Say Instead |
|---|---|
| I think maybe | "Here is what I recommend" |
| We might be able to | "We can" or "We cannot" |
| Hopefully | "The plan is" or state the commitment |
| If possible | (decide if it is possible, then state it) |
| We will try our best | "We will" or "That is outside our scope" |
| It depends | State the range: "Between $149 and $299 depending on page count" |
| To be honest | (just be honest — do not announce it) |
| With all due respect | (say the thing respectfully without the disclaimer) |

## 9. Example Rewrites

Ten before-and-after examples showing how generic copy becomes Athena Studios voice.

### 1. Website Hero Headline

**Before:** "Welcome to Athena Studios — Your Partner in Digital Excellence"
**After:** "Professional websites for local businesses. Custom design. Honest pricing."

**Why:** Removed welcome (filler), removed "partner" (buzzword), removed "digital excellence" (meaningless). Added specifics.

### 2. Email Subject Line

**Before:** "Exciting Opportunity to Enhance Your Online Presence!"
**After:** "Your 28 Google reviews deserve a website"

**Why:** Removed excitement language, removed vague promise. Added a specific, researched detail about the lead.

### 3. Social Media Post

**Before:** "We are passionate about helping small businesses thrive in the digital landscape! Contact us today to learn more about our innovative web solutions."
**After:** "A dentist in Toronto had no website. Her competitor down the street did. We built her one in four days for $299. Now her patients can book online."

**Why:** Replaced passion claim with a real result. Replaced "innovative web solutions" with what actually happened.

### 4. FAQ Answer

**Before:** "At Athena Studios, we pride ourselves on delivering exceptional quality at competitive price points. Our team of experienced professionals leverages cutting-edge technologies to ensure your website exceeds expectations."
**After:** "Your website is custom-built for your business. Not a template. We research your industry, your competitors, and your location before we write a single line of copy or pick a single color."

**Why:** Removed self-congratulation. Removed buzzwords. Described the actual process.

### 5. Proposal Opening

**Before:** "Dear Valued Prospective Client, Thank you for considering Athena Studios for your web design needs. We are thrilled at the opportunity to work with you and look forward to delivering a best-in-class digital experience."
**After:** "James, here is what I recommend based on your business and what we discussed."

**Why:** Used their name. Removed every filler phrase. Got to the point.

### 6. Pricing Statement

**Before:** "Our competitive pricing packages are designed to provide maximum value for businesses of all sizes."
**After:** "Five-page website: $299. Landing page: $149. Hosting: $52/month. That is the full list."

**Why:** Replaced vague promise with actual numbers. Clients should never have to decode a pricing message.

### 7. Objection Response

**Before:** "We completely understand your concern! Rest assured that our team is committed to delivering exceptional results. We have helped countless businesses just like yours achieve their goals online."
**After:** "Fair concern. Here is how we handle it: you see a demo page for your business before you pay anything. If you do not like it, you walk away."

**Why:** Acknowledged the concern, stated the specific safeguard, moved on. No begging.

### 8. Follow-Up Email

**Before:** "Hi there! I hope you're doing well. I just wanted to follow up on our previous conversation and see if you've had a chance to think about our exciting proposal. We'd love the opportunity to work together!"
**After:** "James — did you get a chance to look at the demo page? Here is the link again: [URL]. Let me know if anything needs adjusting."

**Why:** Used their name. Removed every filler phrase. Gave them a reason to reply (the demo link).

### 9. Instagram Caption

**Before:** "Another stunning website by our talented team! We are so proud of this beautiful design. Check out our portfolio for more amazing work! #WebDesign #DigitalMarketing #SmallBiz #Entrepreneur #WebDev #Inspiration #Creative"
**After:** "New build: Mexican restaurant in Miami. Menu gallery, reservation form, private event inquiry page. $299. Live in four days. #WebDesign #LocalBusiness #RestaurantWebsite"

**Why:** Replaced self-praise with facts. Reduced hashtag count. Listed what the client actually got.

### 10. Website Services Description

**Before:** "Our holistic approach to web design combines innovative technology with creative excellence to deliver seamless, best-in-class digital experiences that empower your business to thrive in today's competitive landscape."
**After:** "We build custom websites for local businesses. Every page is designed for your specific industry, your city, and your customers. The goal: your phone rings more."

**Why:** Removed every forbidden word. Replaced abstractions with specifics. Ended with the client benefit.

## 10. Quality Checklist

Run every piece of Athena Studios content through this checklist before publishing.

### Must Pass (all required)

- [ ] Does it lead with the client's benefit, not our capability?
- [ ] Is it free of every word and phrase listed in Section 8 (Forbidden Phrases)?
- [ ] Does it avoid mentioning AI, automation, algorithms, or autonomous systems?
- [ ] Would a 45-year-old dentist with no tech background understand every sentence?
- [ ] Is the price visible if this is a sales or proposal context?
- [ ] Does it reference something specific about the client, their industry, or their location?
- [ ] Does it end with a clear single call to action or a question?

### Should Pass (strongly recommended)

- [ ] Is it under the word limit for its channel? (150 for cold email, 80 for follow-up, 200 for client email, 100 for Instagram caption)
- [ ] Does it use "you" and "your" more than "we" and "our"?
- [ ] Is every claim backed by a number, a name, or a specific detail?
- [ ] Is the tone calibrated to the right point on the spectrum (Section 7)?
- [ ] Would the content still make sense if you removed every adjective?
- [ ] Does the subject line reference something specific, not something generic?
- [ ] Is there exactly one idea per paragraph?

### Red Flags (rewrite immediately)

- [ ] Opens with "I hope this email finds you well" or any variation
- [ ] Contains the word "innovative," "seamless," "leverage," or "synergy"
- [ ] Uses "AI-powered" or references any internal technology
- [ ] Contains more than three exclamation marks in the entire piece
- [ ] Uses "small business" instead of "local business"
- [ ] Has a paragraph longer than four sentences
- [ ] Describes features without connecting them to a business outcome
- [ ] Contains hedging language ("we might," "hopefully," "if possible")
- [ ] Uses a generic greeting ("Dear Sir/Madam," "To whom it may concern")
- [ ] Ends without a clear next step
